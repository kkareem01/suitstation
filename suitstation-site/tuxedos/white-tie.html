The tailcoat is intentionally cut so that the two front halves only meet at one point and never close — the buttons are decorative. The design forces the waistcoat to show through the V opening, which is exactly the point: white tie is built around showcasing the white piqué waistcoat and shirt bib as a vertical highlight. Trying to ‘close’ a tailcoat is the most common rookie mistake we see, and it instantly kills the silhouette. We walk through this at fitting so no one tries it on the floor of the Fox Theatre and panics.
Piqué (also called marcella) is a textured cotton weave with a fine waffle pattern that holds its shape stiffly even after starch softens — that crisp, slightly raised look is what reads as white-tie at twenty paces. The shirt bib, wing collar, waistcoat, and bowtie are all traditionally piqué for that reason. Plain cotton or satin substitutes look limp and instantly downgrade the outfit to costume territory. We source our white-tie components specifically in piqué for Hall County clients heading to symphony galas in Atlanta or destination state events.
