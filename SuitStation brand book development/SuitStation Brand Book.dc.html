SuitStation outfits men who are going somewhere. The identity borrows the discipline of the railway — rules, gauges, timetables — and dresses it in the manners of the tailor's shop: ink on paper, a single measure of brass, and nothing filled that a fine line can carry.

03 — The Logotype

One name, two directions.

Direction A — The Station Sans
SuıtStation
Your original sketch, refined. A geometric sans with the tittle of the first “i” cut as a four-in-hand knot. Confident at distance; built for fascia signage, digital, and the platform.
Direction B — The Tailor's Serif
SuıtStation
The same knot, set in the house serif with the knot taken in brass. Quieter, closer to the cloth; built for print, labels, and the stationery drawer.
Recommendation: carry both. A leads wherever the name must be read in passing; B leads wherever it is held in the hand.

09 — Typography

A serif that stands, a serif that serves.

Display & headings — Cormorant Garamond
The 6:18 to elegance departs daily.
Body — Lora
A suit should arrive the way a good train does: on time, without fuss, and exactly as promised. Body copy is set justified at a comfortable measure, leading kept generous, emphasis carried by italics and weight — never by a change of face.

10 — Patterns & Graphic Devices

Cloth and track.

D-01 · The Track Rule
Section divider and signature rule. Ink by default; brass for ceremony.
D-02 · Pinstripe
The cloth itself: 1 px stripes, 10 px apart. Lining for card backs, bags, and box interiors.
D-03 · The Perforation
Tear-line from the ticket hall. Separates a stub — a price, a date, a size — from the body.
D-04 · Signal Dots
Progress and place: one brass lamp among ink rings. Steppers, size runs, fitting stages.
One device per surface. The devices are punctuation, not wallpaper — pinstripe alone may run edge to edge.
